# State of the art: convincing close-up details

Status: search of the 2026-10-21, made after the rock flight judgment of stage D, "does not perform well". Synthesis of three researches: hybrid materials and techniques, grass, rock and micro-relief.

## The conclusion

**No engine that looks realistic close up draws the floor with pixel-only noise.** All make the same separation:

- **Procedural rules decide where** each material goes: climate, slope, curvature, altitude. That's what our step C already does.
- **Sample materials decide what it looks like:** small seamless textures, scanned or generated by a Substance graph. Colour, relief, roughness and hollow are correlated there.
- **Geometry carries what stands:** grass, pebbles, rocks, cliffs.

**Realistic procedure exists, but it is hybrid.** Either he relies on scans, or he generates his sample textures once, with neat recipes, and then repeats them without a visible pattern.

## Why our floor looks fake

- **Detail lives only in normal:** no hollow that darkens, no shadow, no parallax, no silhouette. Hence the appearance of plaster.
- **Color, embossing and roughness come from noise functions separately,** without any link between them. In a real rock, everything is correlated: the crack is hollow, dark and matte at the same time.
- **The gradient noise is round and isotropic.** The rock is made of flat faces, sharp edges and splinters.
- **The strata are contour lines of a noise,** not inclined planes.
- **The grass under 10 m, they are strands.** No texture replaces them in the raster view.

## What others do

| Engine | Floor close up |
|---|---|
| Star Citizen, Planet Tech v4/v5 | Climate chooses biome. Material sets built in Substance, calibrated on scans, mixed by height, with parallax. Rocks and cliffs in placed objects |
| Outerra | Materials chosen by altitude, slope and curvature; three small textures mixed by a noise fractal against repetition |
| SpaceEngine | Table of 128 seamless textures, selected by altitude, latitude and slope, mixed by height |
| Horizon Zero Dawn | Placement on GPU, deterministic and stable, of more than 100 000 objects for 0,25 ms |
| Ghost of Tsushima | Each blade of grass generated on GPU at each frame; textures and placement by rules |
| Far Cry 4 and 5 | Virtual texture cached around the camera; cliffs in objects, terrain movement |
| Unreal Engine 5 | Height-blend layers, virtual texture, tessellation movement, procedural placement |

**The lesson of Elite Dangerous:** Without a technique against repetition, players see the pattern of textures.

## Useful techniques

### Repeat a texture without it being visible

- **Hexagonal paving,** Mikkelsen 2022. A grid of hexagons, each with random offset and rotation, mixed with accented weights. 3 readings, no precalculation, normals turned correctly. **Best choice by default.**
- **Histogram-preserving blend,** Heitz and Neyret 2018. Same idea, exact contrast, but you need a precalculation per texture and the result is limited to textures without clear shapes.
- **All this is anchored on the planet:** cells are hashed on whole clues, so nothing slips. Our exact network indices around the 64 m anchor are suitable as such.

### Generate realistic textures at startup

- **A graph of nodes in the Substance,** brought into compute and executed once at startup, like our atmosphere tables: pattern distribution, slope blur, directional deformation, cells, cracks, erosion.
- **It produces seamless texture tables,** color, normal, height and roughness in 1K to 2K, by material and by planet seed.
- **Realistic: high for rock, cracks, gravel, sand and snow; medium for earth and litter.** The cost to image is that of ordinary textures.
- **Open reference:** Material Maker GLSL nodes.

### Better rock, even procedural

- **One height field per material,** with its analytic gradient. The normal, the hollows, the thin shadows and the color are derived from it. It is this consistency that gives the impression of matter.
- **Blocks, not bumps:** The Voronoi cell identifier gives each block a randomly inclined flat face, with chamfered edges. Plates with split edges are obtained.
- **strata in relief:** inclined planes, in grades, where the hard layers advance and the soft ones retreat.
- **Erosion:** Clay John's erosion filter, refined to 2023 and then to 2026, traces branched ravines along the slope. The fBm derived from Inigo Quilez gives a cheap erosion.
- **Colour follows shape:** dust in the hollows, dark runs in the direction of the slope, lichens on the upward curved faces, moss in the hollows.

### The lighting that makes the volume

- **The hollows:** the full relief less the smooth relief, almost free.
- **Fine shadows,** in the Call of Duty WWII style: the occlusion changes into a cone that cuts out direct light. Few operations per pixel.
- **Contact shadows,** screen by screen: Bend Studio’s code for Days Gone is public. He captures shadows to the centimeter.
- **The roughness varies in the recesses.** It counts more than shine.

### The real relief, in geometry

- **Our mesh already has grid cells of a few centimeters** under a camera at 1 m from the ground. Adding the height field of materials to the complexplanet relief gives real hollows, real shadows and real silhouettes.
- **This is the extra octave you were talking about,** filtered to 2 grid cells like the others. The benchmark will measure its jitter and its mutation.
- **The parallax** only makes sense with texture heights, so with a graph at startup or scans. It costs dozens of readings per pixel and does not change the silhouette.

### The objects sown

- **Pebbles, stones, rocks:** fixed squares on the planet by size class, about 25 cm, 2 m and 16 m. The hash of the box gives existence, position, rotation, size and variant.
- **10 to 30 rock shapes,** generated at start from spheres deformed by a noise or fractured blocks. Each rock is buried from 20 to 40 % to hide the contact.
- **The cliffs:** no published engine makes them with the relief alone. All add objects.

### The neuronal

- **Nothing beats today** the example textures with hexagonal paving, in realism by millisecond.
- **The only useful use:** train offline, then generate on startup.

## The three ways

| Way | Principe | Realism | Concept |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Improved procedure** | Unified height field, blocks, strata relief, erosion, hollows and thin shadows, pixel | Medium to good | Intact |
| **B. Procedure generated at startup** | Node graph in compute at startup, texture arrays, hexagonal paving, height blending | Good, close to Star Citizen for rock, sand and snow | Intact: no files, a different planet per seed |
| **C. Hybrid with scans** | Royalty free scans, Poly Haven and ambientCG in CC0, hexagonal paving, chosen by our masks | The best, especially for soil and litter | Drops the rule “no image loaded”, for a few MB |

**In the three tracks,** the grass is in geometry, the pebbles and rocks are sown, and the contact and fine shadows are added.

## Recommendation

**The track B, built on A.** It is the most faithful to the concept and achieves a good realism.

1. **The unified height field of the rock,** with blocks, strata in relief, hollows and fine shadows. This is the fastest gain against the coating aspect, and the recipe of the B-way graph follows from it.
2. **The grass blade in geometry,** according to revised `spec-herbe.md`.
3. **Starting graph and hexagonal paving,** for rock, sand, snow and gravel.
4. **The material embossing added to the mesh,** plus contact shadows.
5. **Pebbles and rocks sown.**

**A standard, without giving up the procedural:** keep two or three scans CC0 out of the engine, as a visual reference to judge our materials side by side. The C-way remains a gateway if the soil and litter remain too poor.
